# If dark matter is a residual Sl relic, where is the recoil?

If the Sl transition leaves a neutral relic sector, then the obvious question is whether that relic sector can interact with ordinary matter at all.

The conservative answer is: maybe only through a rare nuclear-recoil portal.

A possible detector process is:

```text
R_Sl + Xe -> R_Sl + Xe
```

This would be a nuclear recoil, not an electron recoil. A fast electron can ionize xenon, but it cannot easily kick the whole xenon nucleus by hundreds of keV. A heavy neutral projectile is more plausible.

But this is not a claim that any current xenon event proves dark matter. One candidate recoil is background until repeated and confirmed.

The important constraint is astrophysical: if the relic interacts often enough to cool, heat gas, lose angular momentum, or settle into accretion disks, it should already be visible. So the allowed portal cannot be broad or dissipative.

The relic may occasionally kick a nucleus.

It must not behave like gas.

That is the narrow window.
