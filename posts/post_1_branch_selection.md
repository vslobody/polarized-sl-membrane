# Why baryon asymmetry may be a branch-selection problem, not net creation

The usual phrase is "matter-antimatter asymmetry." It sounds like the universe created more matter than antimatter.

This conjecture asks a different question: what if the asymmetry is local to our branch, while the deeper global structure remains balanced?

The proposed picture is:

```text
balanced deeper state -> matter branch + conjugate branch
```

Our branch contains matter-oriented residuals. The conjugate branch contains the opposite orientation. Globally, baryon number can remain zero.

This is not an ordinary matter/antimatter domain model. There are no nearby antimatter galaxies hidden across empty space. Local QED remains intact: positrons can be produced and annihilated inside our branch.

The key proposed clock is the observed baryon-to-photon ratio:

```text
eta_B ~ 6e-10
```

Before Sl condensation, annihilation continues. When Sl condenses, the surviving matter-oriented and conjugate-oriented populations are branch-separated. That stopping time gives an estimated transition scale near:

```text
T_c ~ 38--40 MeV
```

The conjecture is not established physics. The value is that it gives failure conditions: if the transition timing, gauge neutrality, or relic sector cannot be made consistent, the model dies.
